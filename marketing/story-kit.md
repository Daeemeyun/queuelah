## What it is

**QueueLah** is a free iOS app that shows live, crowd-reported queue statuses at Singapore hawker centres on a map. Anyone can open the app and browse — no account, no ads, no paywall. Users who sign up can report queue lengths and earn points, badges and a spot on the leaderboard.

**One-liner:** *Queue less. Eat more.* — see the hawker queue before you walk over.

## The problem

The lunchtime gamble. You have a one-hour lunch window. You walk ten minutes to your favourite stall at Maxwell, and only then discover the 30-minute queue. Queueing is practically a national sport in Singapore — QueueLah just lets you see the queue before committing your lunch hour to it.

## The founder story

Damien Toh is a solo Singaporean indie developer. He got tired of losing his lunch hour to queues he couldn't see, so he built the fix himself — nights and weekends, one person, no funding, no ads, free for everyone. (Bonus colour: Apple rejected the first submission over a blank-screen bug caused by a missing environment variable in the production build — a classic indie-dev war story, happily resolved.)

## Why it launches narrow (the interesting strategy bit)

Crowd-sourced apps face a chicken-and-egg problem: the map is only useful if people report, and people only report if the map feels alive. So QueueLah is deliberately launching **dense, not wide** — concentrating first users at **Maxwell Food Centre, Lau Pa Sat and Amoy Street Food Centre**, where the CBD lunch crowd feels the queue pain most. Coverage grows wherever users do.

## Key facts (all true, please quote accurately)

- Free. No ads. No account needed to browse.
- iOS only at launch (iPhone). Android: not yet — no promised date.
- Crowd-sourced: queue statuses come from real users, shown near-real-time on a map.
- Gamified contribution: points, badges, leaderboard for reporters.
- Strongest coverage: Maxwell, Lau Pa Sat, Amoy (CBD). **Not** Singapore-wide on day one — and we say so.
- Built solo by a Singaporean indie developer.
